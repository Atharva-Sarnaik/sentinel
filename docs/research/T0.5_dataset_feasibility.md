## 1. Executive Summary

Sentinel monitors spacecraft telemetry through four semi-autonomous specialist agents—**Power**, **Thermal**, **Attitude and Orbit Control (AOCS)**, and **Communications & Data Handling (CDH)**—coordinated by a deterministic fusion engine, an LLM reasoner, and a grounding verifier. A foundational prerequisite (Risk R1) is establishing whether public benchmark datasets provide physically grounded, literature-backed telemetry channels that can be reliably partitioned into these four operational subsystems.

This investigation evaluates the two primary candidate benchmark datasets documented in **ADR-0001**:
1. **NASA SMAP (Soil Moisture Active Passive)** satellite telemetry benchmark
2. **NASA MSL (Mars Science Laboratory / Curiosity Rover)** telemetry benchmark

### Key Findings
1. **Physical Authenticity & Benchmark Lineage:** Both datasets, compiled by Kyle Hundman et al. (NASA JPL, KDD 2018), represent authentic flight telemetry with ground-truth anomalies verified by JPL Incident Surprise Anomaly (ISA) reports. Both are distributed as 2-D NumPy arrays (`.npy`) accompanied by sequence-level annotations in `labeled_anomalies.csv`.
2. **Spacecraft Dynamics vs. Rover Dynamics:** NASA SMAP represents an authentic Earth-orbiting satellite in sun-synchronous low Earth orbit (685 km), exhibiting genuine orbital periodicity, eclipse power transitions, radiative thermal dissipation, and reaction wheel attitude management. NASA MSL represents a planetary surface rover whose operations are fundamentally command-driven (mobility mechanisms, robotic arm articulation, and surface science instruments).
3. **Critical Finding – Independent Storage & Lack of Shared Timestamps:** In the NASA benchmark repository, each telemetry channel was extracted and packaged as an **independent operational entity** rather than a synchronized wide bus log. Different channels have **differing sequence lengths** (e.g., in the test split, `P-1` has 8,505 timesteps, `P-2` has 8,209 timesteps, and `T-1` has 8,612 timesteps). Crucially, **these independent streams lack verified shared wall-clock timestamps or Mission Elapsed Time (MET)**. Truncating streams to an arbitrary minimum length or padding them creates a superficial dimensional match, but does **not** establish temporal alignment or physical synchrony.
4. **Critical Finding – Mission Origin of Canonical Channels:** Cross-referencing `labeled_anomalies.csv` reveals that 12 of the 14 channels in `libs/config/channel_map.yaml` originate from **SMAP** (`P-1`..`P-4`, `T-1`..`T-3`, `A-1`..`A-4`, and `D-1`), whereas `C-1` and `C-2` originate from **MSL**. Merging Mars rover telemetry with Earth satellite telemetry in a single multi-channel window couples two distinct spacecraft across different planetary environments.
5. **Leakage Distinction:** A clear separation must be maintained between **runtime chronological leakage** (prevented in Sentinel's data plane by strictly non-overlapping synthetic timestamp ranges and verified by `tests/leakage/test_leakage.py`) and **inherited benchmark preprocessing leakage** (the original Telemanom benchmark pre-scaled telemetry values using global test-set min/max, an inherent characteristic of the public dataset).
6. **Architectural Recommendation:** Sentinel should adopt **independent channel-level training and evaluation** as the safe, rigorous initial baseline. Synchronized multi-channel windowing within a single specialist must be treated as future work requiring external timestamp and synchronization evidence from raw mission archives.

## 2. Dataset Overview

### 2.1 NASA SMAP (Soil Moisture Active Passive)
- **Mission Profile:** NASA Earth observation satellite launched on January 31, 2015, operating in a near-polar sun-synchronous orbit at an altitude of 685 km.
- **Data Provenance:** Extracted from the JPL Advanced Telemetry and Range Management System. Published by Hundman et al. (KDD 2018) via the `telemanom` open-source framework and NASA PO.DAAC.
- **Dataset Dimensions:** 55 telemetry channels. Training files consist of nominal operational sequences (~2,000 to ~3,000 timesteps per channel); test files consist of incident and anomaly sequences (~5,000 to ~8,600 timesteps per channel).
- **Physical Context:** Orbital period of ~98.5 minutes creates predictable cyclical dynamics: solar array charging during sunlight, battery discharge during eclipse, thermal radiator heating/cooling cycles, and reaction wheel momentum dumping.

### 2.2 NASA MSL (Mars Science Laboratory / Curiosity Rover)
- **Mission Profile:** Planetary exploration rover launched in November 2011, landing in Gale Crater on Mars in August 2012.
- **Data Provenance:** Telemetry extracted from rover testbeds and operational flight logs. Published alongside SMAP by Hundman et al. (2018).
- **Dataset Dimensions:** 27 telemetry channels. Training files contain ~1,500 to ~2,200 timesteps; test files contain ~2,000 to ~2,300 timesteps.
- **Physical Context:** Governed by diurnal Martian sol cycles and discrete telecommand sequences sent from Earth. Subsystems reflect surface mobility (`M-1`..`M-7`), robotic mechanisms, MMRTG power (`P-10`..`P-15`), and radio communication passes.

### 2.3 Secondary & Fallback Benchmarks
- **ESA-ADB (ESA Anomaly Detection Benchmark):** Published on Zenodo by the European Space Agency (ESOC/Airbus/KP Labs). Contains multi-year telemetry from large ESA spacecraft (>1.5 billion points). High-dimensional multi-mission benchmark, but significantly larger and computationally intensive.
- **OPSSAT-AD:** Compact benchmark extracted from ESA's OPS-SAT CubeSat, featuring 9 continuous sensor channels (magnetometers and photodiodes). Suitable for edge testing on resource-constrained platforms.

## 4. Data Structure Analysis

### 4.1 On-Disk Organization
Both datasets share an identical hierarchical directory layout:

```text
data/raw/{DATASET}/
├── train/
│   ├── {chan_id}.npy          # Shape: (n_train_steps, n_features)
│   └── ...
├── test/
│   ├── {chan_id}.npy          # Shape: (n_test_steps, n_features)
│   └── ...
└── labeled_anomalies.csv      # Ground-truth incident intervals
```

### 4.2 Array Formatting & Feature Roles
Within each `.npy` file:
- **Matrix Dimension:** 2-D array of shape $(N, D)$ where $N$ is the number of time samples and $D$ is the feature count.
- **Column 0 ($[:, 0]$):** The **primary target telemetry signal** (e.g., Bus Voltage for `P-1`).
- **Columns 1 to $D-1$ ($[:, 1:]$):** Contextual engineering telemetry and one-hot encoded telecommands active during the corresponding recording interval. In SMAP, $D=25$ for standard files; in MSL, $D$ ranges between 25 and 55 depending on active telecommand categories.
- **Value Bounds:** Raw values were pre-processed by JPL using min-max scaling to $[-1.0, 1.0]$ based on the global minimum and maximum of the test set.

### 4.3 Ground-Truth Label Specification
Ground-truth anomalies are cataloged in `labeled_anomalies.csv`:

```csv
chan_id,spacecraft,anomaly_sequences,class,num_values
P-1,SMAP,"[[2149, 2349], [4536, 4844], [3539, 3779]]","['contextual', 'contextual', 'contextual']",8505
T-1,SMAP,"[[2399, 3898], [6550, 6585]]","['point', 'contextual']",8612
C-1,MSL,"[[550, 750], [2100, 2210]]","['point', 'contextual']",2264
```

- **Endpoint Convention:** The indices $[start, end]$ are **inclusive** of both bounds per the official Hundman et al. evaluation implementation.
- **Split Separation:** Anomaly sequences are indexed exclusively against the **test** partition. Training files represent nominally healthy spacecraft behavior.

### 4.4 The Temporal Alignment Problem
A critical characteristic of the Telemanom benchmark is that each channel is an **independently extracted time series**:
- Each `.npy` file was extracted around specific operational periods or anomaly investigations.
- `P-1` has 8,505 test timesteps; `P-2` has 8,209 test timesteps; `T-1` has 8,612 test timesteps.
- There are **no shared absolute timestamps** (e.g., UTC, GPS time, or continuous mission elapsed time) linking step $i$ of `P-1` with step $i$ of `P-2`.
- Slicing or truncating `P-1` and `P-2` to an arbitrary common length (e.g., 8,209 steps) does **not** create a synchronized bus log; timestep $i$ in `P-1` may have been recorded during a completely different orbit, day, or season than timestep $i$ in `P-2`.
- Therefore, **multi-channel window merging across independent channel files cannot be physically defended** without external synchronization evidence.

## 5. Adapter Compatibility Analysis

### 5.1 Verification of Implemented Adapter Functions
The telemetry adapter in [services/data_plane/adapter.py](file:///c:/Users/sujal/OneDrive/Documents/Satellite/sentinel/services/data_plane/adapter.py) provides:

1. **`stream_npy_windows()` (Single-Channel Streaming):**
   - **Capability:** Reads 2-D NumPy arrays, extracts column 0 as `float64`, validates finiteness, synthesizes chronological timestamps using `base_timestamp` and `time_step_seconds`, and slices sequences into `TelemetryWindow` contracts.
   - **Status:** **Fully compatible** with both SMAP and MSL `.npy` files. This is the **recommended operational mode** for the benchmark.
2. **`load_smap_labels()` (Metadata Loader):**
   - **Capability:** Parses `labeled_anomalies.csv` for a given `chan_id` and `spacecraft`.
   - **Status:** **Compatible**, provided callers pass the correct `spacecraft` identifier (`"SMAP"` vs. `"MSL"`).
3. **`stream_npy_multi_channel_windows()` (Multi-Channel Merging):**
   - **Capability:** Aggregates multiple named channel files into a single window.
   - **Limitation:** Enforces strict equal row counts (`arr.shape[0] == n_steps`). When applied to raw channel files with mismatched row counts, it correctly raises `ValueError: Timestep count mismatch`.
   - **Architectural Assessment:** While functionally sound for synchronized datasets, this helper **should not be used to merge raw, independently sampled SMAP/MSL channel streams** without a defensible, verified alignment method.

### 5.2 Timestamp Synthesis & Contract Compliance
Because NASA `.npy` benchmark files omit wall-clock timestamps:
- The adapter synthesizes UTC datetimes starting from an arbitrary reference origin (default `2024-01-01 00:00:00 UTC`).
- For test splits, the base timestamp is offset by the training sequence duration:
  $$\text{test\_base\_timestamp} = \text{train\_base\_timestamp} + (N_{\text{train}} \times \Delta t)$$
- This guarantees strictly increasing chronological order, satisfying the non-negotiable requirement of **ADR-0002** and verified by `tests/leakage/test_leakage.py`.

Sentinel parameterizes four specialist detectors according to [libs/config/channel_map.yaml](file:///c:/Users/sujal/OneDrive/Documents/Satellite/sentinel/libs/config/channel_map.yaml). The compatibility breakdown per subsystem is detailed below.

### 6.1 Power Subsystem Specialist

- **Spacecraft Origin:** **SMAP** (Verified in `labeled_anomalies.csv`)

  - `P-2`: Solar Array Current (Amps) — test length: 8,209
  - `P-3`: Battery State of Charge (%) — test length: 8,505

They must be modeled as independent channel-level detectors under the Power specialist umbrella.

### 6.2 Thermal Subsystem Specialist

  - `T-3`: Battery Module Temperature (°C) — test length: 8,579
- **Feasibility:** **VERY HIGH (for single-channel detectors)**. Reflects orbital solar exposure and dissipation dynamics.
- **Synchronization Constraint:** Differing sequence lengths confirm independent extractions; models should evaluate each thermal stream independently.

### 6.3 Attitude & Orbit Control (AOCS) Specialist
- **Configured Channels:** `A-1`, `A-2`, `A-3`, `A-4`
- **Spacecraft Origin:** **SMAP** (Verified in `labeled_anomalies.csv`)
- **Documented Physical Grounding:**
  - `A-1`: Reaction Wheel Speed X (RPM) — test length: ~8,000
  - `A-2`: Reaction Wheel Speed Y (RPM) — test length: 7,914
  - `A-3`: Gyroscope Rate Z (deg/s) — test length: 8,205
  - `A-4`: Star Tracker Quaternion W — test length: 8,080
- **Feasibility:** **VERY HIGH (for single-channel detectors)**. Captures reaction wheel momentum loading and attitude control excursions.
- **Synchronization Constraint:** Independent extraction lengths require channel-wise modeling rather than synchronized vector input.

### 6.4 Communications & Data Handling (CDH) Specialist
- **Configured Channels:** `C-1`, `C-2`, `D-1`
- **Spacecraft Origin:** **MIXED / HYBRID**
  - `C-1`: MSL Telemetry Stream (Command link / receiver) — test length: 2,264 (`spacecraft="MSL"`)
  - `C-2`: MSL Telemetry Stream (Transponder output) — test length: 2,051 (`spacecraft="MSL"`)
  - `D-1`: SMAP Instrument/Data Buffer Fill Level — test length: 8,509 (`spacecraft="SMAP"`)
- **Feasibility & Constraints:**
  - `C-1` and `C-2` belong to the **MSL Mars rover**, while `D-1` belongs to the **SMAP satellite**. Merging them into a single window implies temporal coupling between a rover on Mars and an orbiter around Earth, which is physically impossible.
  - Calling `load_smap_labels()` for `C-1` with `spacecraft="SMAP"` returns no labels; `spacecraft="MSL"` must be passed explicitly.
- **Assessment of Pure SMAP Alternative (`D-1`, `D-2`, `D-3`):**
  - SMAP contains channel entities `D-1` through `D-16` in `labeled_anomalies.csv`.
  - While `D-1` is historically documented as data/buffer fill level, **the physical meanings of `D-2` and `D-3` remain UNVERIFIED** without authoritative NASA JPL flight telemetry dictionaries.
  - Re-assigning `D-2` and `D-3` to specific communications metrics (e.g., downlink rate or transponder power) would be an ungrounded assumption. Therefore, any pure SMAP CDH mapping remains **UNVERIFIED** and should not be adopted without primary mission documentation.

## 7. Leakage and Data Quality Risks

### 7.1 Distinguishing Runtime vs. Inherited Benchmark Leakage
A critical scientific distinction must be maintained between runtime leakage and inherited preprocessing leakage:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        DATA LEAKAGE CATEGORIES                         │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│   RUNTIME CHRONOLOGICAL LEAKAGE   │  INHERITED PREPROCESSING LEAKAGE   │
│   (Controlled by Sentinel)        │  (Inherent to Public Benchmark)    │
├───────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Cause: Test windows overlapping │ • Cause: Raw values pre-scaled     │
│   or preceding training data.     │   using global test-set min/max    │
│ • Severity: High (Invalidates ML) │   during dataset publication.      │
│ • Sentinel Defense: Synthetic     │ • Sentinel Defense: Documented     │
│   timestamp offsets + permanent   │   honestly as benchmark property.  │
│   CI regression suite             │   Sentinel does NOT fit normalisers│
│   (test_leakage.py).              │   on test data.                    │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Chronological / Look-Ahead Leakage (Sentinel Runtime):**
   - **Description:** Exposing future or test-set telemetry points to models during training, or shuffling windows across the split boundary.
   - **Sentinel Mitigation:** ADR-0002 strictly mandates chronological windowing. Sentinel's adapter calculates:
     $$\text{test\_base\_timestamp} \ge \text{train\_base\_timestamp} + (N_{\text{train}} \times \Delta t)$$
     ensuring that every test window strictly succeeds all training windows. This is permanently guarded by `tests/leakage/test_leakage.py`.
2. **Preprocessing Scaling Leakage (Inherited from Benchmark):**
   - **Description:** As documented in the official Telemanom README (`khundman/telemanom/README.md`) and Hundman et al. (2018), raw telemetry points were pre-scaled into $[-1.0, 1.0]$ using the global minimum and maximum of the test set:
     $$\tilde{x}_t = 2 \cdot \frac{x_t - \min(X_{\text{test}})}{\max(X_{\text{test}}) - \min(X_{\text{test}})} - 1$$
   - **Impact:** This is an intrinsic characteristic of the public SMAP/MSL benchmark data files used across the literature (OmniAnomaly, TranAD, MTAD-GAT).
   - **Sentinel Mitigation:** Sentinel does **not** introduce additional scaling leakage. The specialist training pipeline in `services/specialists/train.py` feeds raw adapter output directly into `LSTMAETemplate`, bypassing feature plane normalizers and preventing double-normalization.

### 7.2 Multi-Channel Merging Risks
- **Artificial Temporal Coupling:** Forcing independently sampled channels into a single `TelemetryWindow` assumes that index $i$ in channel A corresponds to index $i$ in channel B. Without synchronized timestamps, this assumption is unverified and introduces spurious correlations into multivariate models.
- **Length Inconsistency:** `stream_npy_multi_channel_windows()` raises `ValueError` when channel lengths differ. Slicing to the minimum length resolves the runtime error, but does **not** resolve the underlying temporal desynchronization.

## 8. Final Recommendation

### 8.1 Primary Dataset: NASA SMAP
**Decision:** **Adopt NASA SMAP as the Primary Benchmark Dataset.**
- **Justification:**
  1. SMAP is an authentic Earth-orbiting satellite matching Sentinel's explicit mission scope.
  2. 12 of the 14 channels in Sentinel's canonical configuration originate from SMAP.
  3. Extensive test sequences (5,000 to 8,612 timesteps per channel) provide strong statistical power for anomaly detection evaluation.
  4. Real orbital physics validate cross-subsystem corroboration rules (e.g., Power bus voltage drop coupled with thermal radiator temperature decrease during orbital eclipse).

### 8.2 Secondary Dataset: NASA MSL
**Decision:** **Retain NASA MSL for Cross-Mission Evaluation.**
- **Justification:**
  1. MSL provides **cross-mission evaluation** across a completely different mission class (a planetary surface rover).
  2. It evaluates whether Sentinel's detector architecture can generalize to different telemetry dynamics (command-driven surface mechanisms).
  3. It does **not** serve as direct validation of identical satellite subsystem behavior, because a surface rover operates under distinct physical and operational constraints.

### 8.3 Recommended Detection Architecture: Channel-Level Specialists
Rather than attempting to force unaligned channel streams into a single synchronized `TelemetryWindow`:
1. **Safe Initial Approach (Recommended):**
   - Train and evaluate specialist autoencoders at the **independent channel or entity level** (e.g., an independent detector for `P-1`, an independent detector for `P-2`, etc.), matching standard benchmark methodology in Hundman et al. (2018) and OmniAnomaly.
   - Each specialist aggregates the anomaly opinions of its constituent channel detectors (e.g., the Power Specialist reports an anomaly if any of its constituent power channel detectors flag an incident).
2. **Future Work (Synchronized Multi-Channel Windows):**
   - Generating a true multi-variate, multi-channel `TelemetryWindow` within a single specialist requires external timestamp and synchronization evidence from primary NASA mission archives or a dataset with explicit shared timestamps (such as ESA-ADB).

### 8.4 Communications Channel Configuration
- **Current Baseline:** Maintain `C-1` (MSL), `C-2` (MSL), and `D-1` (SMAP) as a composite cross-mission benchmark. When evaluating labels, pass `spacecraft="MSL"` for `C-1`/`C-2` and `spacecraft="SMAP"` for `D-1`.
- **Pure SMAP Mapping Status:** Alternative mappings (such as `D-1`, `D-2`, `D-3`) remain **UNVERIFIED** without authoritative NASA flight telemetry dictionaries. They must not be treated as confirmed physical communications telemetry.
